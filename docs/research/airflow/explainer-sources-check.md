## 2. Stam (1999) and Zuo and Chen (2009)

The "How it works" claim is: "This is Fast Fluid Dynamics, which Stam described for animation in 1999 and Zuo and Chen applied to rooms in 2009." It follows the description of each step: carry the flow along, push it with buoyancy and fans, add eddy viscosity, then correct the pressure.

**Stam (1999).** Verified citation: Jos Stam, "Stable Fluids", Proceedings of the 26th Annual Conference on Computer Graphics and Interactive Techniques (SIGGRAPH '99), ACM Press, pp. 121-128, 1999, DOI 10.1145/311535.311548. Sources: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/311535.311548 and the paper, read at https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/public_user/stam/reality/Research/pdf/ns.pdf.
- What the claim rests on: Stam's paper is an unconditionally stable Navier-Stokes solver written for animation in computer graphics. Its abstract and its CR category ("Animation") say so. Each time step does four things in order: add force, advect (semi-Lagrangian back-tracing), diffuse (implicit), and project (a Poisson solve that makes the field divergence-free). So the paper supports "Stam described [the method] for animation in 1999".
- What the paper does not support: Stam never uses the name "Fast Fluid Dynamics", which comes from Zuo and Chen. The explainer's order "moves the air, pushes it, smooths it, then fixes the pressure" also differs slightly from Stam's, which adds force before advecting.
- Explainer-style entry: Stam, “Stable Fluids,” <i>Proceedings of SIGGRAPH 99</i>, ACM, 121–128 (1999): the solver each step follows, written for animation. Forces are added, the flow is carried along itself, smoothed, and then made free of divergence by a pressure solve. (Link: https://doi.org/10.1145/311535.311548.)

**Zuo and Chen (2009).** Verified citation: Wangda Zuo, Qingyan Chen, "Real-time or faster-than-real-time simulation of airflow in buildings", Indoor Air 19(1), 33-44, February 2009, DOI 10.1111/j.1600-0668.2008.00559.x (PMID 19191926). Sources: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/j.1600-0668.2008.00559.x, the PubMed abstract, and the authors' manuscript at https://engineering.purdue.edu/~yanchen/paper/2009-4.pdf, which is titled "Real time or ..." without the hyphen.
- What the claim rests on: the paper names the method. It says "Stam (1999) developed a Fast Fluid Dynamics (FFD) method for computer games". It then applies the method to four basic building flows: turbulent flow in a plane channel, forced convection in a ventilated room (Restivo 1979), natural convection in a tall cavity (Betts and Bokhari 2000), and mixed convection in a ventilated room (Blay et al. 1992). It finds FFD about 50 times faster than CFD but less accurate. So "Zuo and Chen applied [it] to rooms in 2009" is supported.
- A point to know: Zuo and Chen found that "the FFD with the laminar assumption has the best overall performance". They also tried two turbulence treatments, a constant νt = 100ν and the Chen-Xu zero-equation model. Neither predicted the main recirculation of the forced-convection room well, and the authors suggest, as a "possible reason", that both over-estimate the turbulent viscosity. The explainer's eddy viscosity that "grows where the air shears fast" therefore does not come from this paper, and the paper does not endorse it. The explainer's sentence does not claim that it does.
- Explainer-style entry: Zuo and Chen, “Real-time or faster-than-real-time simulation of airflow in buildings,” <i>Indoor Air</i> 19, 33–44 (2009): Stam’s method, which they named fast fluid dynamics, applied to airflow in rooms and checked against measured flows. It was about 50 times faster than conventional CFD but less accurate. (Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0668.2008.00559.x.)
