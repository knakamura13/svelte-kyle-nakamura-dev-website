## Results

Benchmarks on the CPU solver, rerun after the advection limit (issue 13). The cavities and the particulate decay did not move; the free jet and the lowest-wind orifice case did:

- Lid-driven cavity at 128²: 1.80% max and 1.19% rms against Ghia (1.77% and 1.18% before the limit), 2.47% and 1.67% against Botella–Peyret.
- Heated cavity: average Nu within 0.3% of de Vahl Davis at Ra 1e3, 1e4 and 1e5.
- Openings: wind through-flow 95.7% of the orifice formula at 0.06 Pa (96% before, so that test's lower bound is now 95%) and 98.9% at 6 Pa. Stack effect within its bounds.
- Well-mixed decay 99.9% of the analytic rate, and the particulate mass balance closes to 1e-5.
- Window fan: matches the analytic series solution. Free jet: delivered flow 101.2%, decay exponent −0.87, constant K 6.21, 2% above the published 5.7 to 6.1 (100.1%, −0.93 and 6.15 before the limit; 98.3%, −1.05 and 5.85 originally).
- A closed room at rest (`tests/solver/decay.spec.ts`: studio, every opening shut, no temperature difference, a box fan for 20 s and then 80 s without it), kinetic energy in the grid's own norm: 28 with the fan on, 14 a second after it stops, 0.15 at 40 s and 0.04 at 80 s. Before the limit it was 28, 14, 10 and 19: it fell and grew back (issue 13).

Scenarios, in the CPU solver at 0.2 m cells (`tests/solver/scenario-presets.spec.ts`):

- Cross-ventilation (two-bedroom, 4 m/s west wind): 1.09 m³/s in at the west windows and out at the east ones, and a closed bedroom door cuts that bedroom's flow to zero.
- Stack effect (house, no wind, −5 °C outside): 0.26 m³/s in at the low windows and out at the clerestory (0.31 before the casement limit became 0.8, issue 4), which is 0.87 of the standard two-opening stack formula for the open areas and heights, as it was 0.86 before.
- A fan in the window (two-bedroom, no wind, Air King 9155 on high): 1.03 m³/s, 88% of the rated 1.17 m³/s. The air goes toward the hall and bedroom 1 and leaves by the open window there: mean room speeds 0.32 m/s in the dining room, 0.34 in bedroom 1, 0.24 in the hall, 0.21 in the kitchen and 0.13 in the living room, with the bathroom and bedroom 2 (doors closed) still. A minute after the fan is turned off no room is above 0.1 m/s. The preset's notice and its test say exactly this (it used to say the air crosses the living room).

Stability at finer cells. A heads-up note said the random stress ran only at 0.2 m, while the GPU path mostly runs 0.05 to 0.1 m and the studio and one-bedroom had never run there. Running it found one root cause behind every failure, and a hang risk (issue 1). The random stress now takes `ROBUST_H` (cell size), `ROBUST_TRIALS`, `ROBUST_SEEDS` and `ROBUST_VERBOSE`, and stops a case at the first step the air passes 40 m/s. That is what made fine-cell runs possible: before it, a blown-up case took minutes per step. With the final solver:

- CPU, studio and one-bedroom, five seeds of each random generator (openings and weather changed mid-run; fans and purifiers turned mid-run): all pass at 0.15, 0.1 and 0.075 m. At 0.075 m the fastest air stays under 8 m/s and the divergence under 1e-2 (limit 0.05), which is the slow pressure convergence of issue 1 showing at fine cells.
- CPU, 30 simulated seconds in a 3 m/s wind and in still air at −5 °C: all four plans at 0.2 m (limits 10 m/s and divergence 0.01, passing), and the studio and one-bedroom at 0.15 and 0.1 m, where the fastest air stays under 1.2 m/s and the divergence under 5e-5 after the first 100 steps. The 0.075 m runs of these were not done (about 10 minutes each on the CPU).
- GPU (`STRESS=1 node_modules/.bin/playwright test -c playwright.gpu.config.ts stress.gpu`, under 2 minutes): all four plans, five seeds of each generator, at 0.15, 0.1 and 0.075 m, and at 0.05 m on the studio, plus 600-step runs in wind and in still cold air. All pass, and a rerun on this Mac passed all 14 stress tests. The worst case was the studio's seed 50004 at 0.05 m: 15 m/s and divergence 0.019.
- Five seeds per plan and size are a sample. A wider sweep is `ROBUST_TRIALS=30` on the CPU, or a larger count in the GPU sweep, on an idle machine.
- After the advection limit (issue 13): the GPU sweep above passes again (33 tests with `STRESS=1`, 19 without), and so does the CPU robustness suite at 0.2 m. The CPU stress for the studio and one-bedroom (random openings and weather, random fans and purifiers, and 30 s of wind and of still cold air) passes at 0.15 m and at 0.1 m too; 0.075 m was not repeated.

GPU step cost on the house plan, windows closed, three pressure cycles: 0.2 m 0.64 ms (41k cells), 0.15 m 0.86 ms (98k), 0.1 m 2.05 ms (332k), 0.075 m 5.19 ms (786k); about 10% more when the caller awaits only every fourth step. 2. Done on 2026-10-14 (#46): the CPU worker backend. `WorkerBackend` (`solver/cpu/proxy.ts`) implements `FlowBackend` over messages to a module worker that runs `CpuBackend`, and `chooseBackend` (`sim/backends.ts`) picks it where WebGPU is missing. What it decided, so the next steps need not rediscover it:
   - Every step is acknowledged. `FlowBackend.stepsInFlight` is how many a backend has not finished (absent for the GPU and the in-page CPU, which the session still times). The session gives a backend that reports it at most two unfinished steps (`MAX_IN_FLIGHT`), keeps the simulated time it could not send as owed to the next frame (at most one stable step's worth, the rest is dropped), and sends that as one longer step, so a worker that needs 30 ms a step keeps up at real time instead of being sent a 17 ms step every frame. A worker that is too slow runs slower than real time, and `physics.rate` (averaged over half a second) says by how much. The page's status line adds "Running at 0.6× real time." when the rate is under 85% of what the clock asks, outside the live region so a screen reader is not told every few seconds. The Dock's clock (#52) reads `physics.rate` too, in the same words.
   - Particle steps are one in flight at a time, and those asked for while one is on its way are summed (to at most 0.1 s) into the next, so a worker that is behind is not buried in them. Replies move their arrays to the page (every array a `CpuBackend` snapshot or read returns is a fresh copy), and a request's arrays are copied.
   - The worker is 0.2 m cells (`CPU_CELL`): a step costs 14 to 41 ms there on this Mac (table above), under the 50 ms stable step, so every plan runs at about real time on a fast processor (browser QA below). The GPU stays at 0.15 m. The tuner (step 4) replaces both numbers. The three Scenarios start at five times real time (`clock: { speed: 5 }` in `scenarios.ts`), which the worker cannot reach, so on the worker the page starts with its status line saying how fast it does run.
   - Whether WebGPU works is settled once for the page. No WebGPU or no adapter is a console warning, said once; any other failure to open the device is logged as an error and the page runs on the worker; a device lost mid-run (`GpuBackend.onFailure`, from `GpuContext.onLost`) reports `lost`, the session drops that world at once (a dead backend is never stepped or read again), announces it, and builds the world again on the worker. The air starts from rest, because nothing can be read back from a lost device, and the page stays on the worker for good. A worker that crashes (`crashed`) stops the simulation with an error until the visitor restarts. If a device fails while the solver is built on it, that one world is built on the worker at the GPU's grid (slower than it should be), and the next world is built for the worker.
   - "Cannot run" now means neither WebGPU nor a Web Worker (`BackendUnavailable`, status `unsupported`), which the e2e suite shows with `Worker` removed.
   - The worker is a module worker (`worker.format: 'es'` in `vite.config.ts`), a chunk of its own, so the page's first JavaScript still holds none of the solver.
   - An independent code review of the step found and the step fixed: a world on the worker placed fans against the plan's opening states for its whole life, because `World` relied on an in-thread backend writing the new face fractions into the page's `raster.geometry` as a side effect (the GPU and the in-page CPU did, one update late; a worker only changes its copy), so `World.applyAll` now writes them into its own geometry before it rasterizes the fans, for every backend (a test with a fan in a doorway the visitor opens fails without it: 46 push faces on the worker against 56); replacing a world on the worker logged "Reading the simulation failed" for a read the old worker rejected as it stopped, so only the running world's reads are reported; a build overtaken while the backend was being chosen no longer builds a world; the page leaving no longer logs an error or starts a worker; and the rate starts from the clock's speed after a pause or a speed change, so the page does not call that "slower than real time".
   - Browser QA in `vite dev` by an agent (Firefox, which has no WebGPU; Chromium's headless shell with `navigator.gpu` removed; Chromium with hardware rendering, with the GPU and with `navigator.gpu` removed), 10 s windows per plan, measured before the review's fixes: the page thread is as smooth with the worker as with the GPU where rendering is on the GPU or in Firefox (frames 13 to 17 ms at p95, no long task and no frame over 50 ms for any plan, the house at 20× included), and the headless shell's software rendering janks whatever solver it runs (long tasks to 149 ms, which is drawing and not stepping); the most steps ever unfinished was 2; at 1× the rate was 1.0 for the studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom and 0.98 (Chromium) to 0.9 (Firefox) for the house; the ceilings at the Scenarios' 5× and at 20×, in Chromium with hardware rendering, were 3.5× for the studio, 2.4× for the one-bedroom, 1.7× for the two-bedroom and 1.2× for the house (Firefox 2.7, 1.9, 1.3 and 0.9); a plan swap takes 0.1 s (1.0 to 1.4 s in the software-rendered shell); and 3 to 4 "Reading the simulation failed" console errors came with the swaps, which the review's second finding explains and the fix removed (the e2e test now swaps homes on a worker and expects none). ## Known issues and open decisions

1. Pressure-solve instability: fixed on the CPU and the GPU, with one weak point left. The studio and one-bedroom plans blew up in the CPU solver, and the finer-cell stress found more. Root cause, with a falsifying test (`tests/solver/pressure.spec.ts` fails on the original solver and passes on the fix): the multigrid's coarse pressure operator halved interior faces. For 2×2×2 aggregation with piecewise-constant transfer that is the rediscretised operator, and the coarse correction came out about twice too large, so smooth pressure errors flipped sign every cycle. A solve with an odd number of cycles then fed a sign-flipping pressure into the next step. That is why the pressure flipped sign every step, and why one-bedroom seed 1004 at 0.15 m ran away with 3 and 5 cycles and not with 4 and 6. The fix sums the interface faces (the Galerkin operator, `coarsen` in `solver/cpu/pressure.ts`).
   - With it, seed 1004 is stable at 1 to 6 cycles at 0.12, 0.15 and 0.2 m. So are studio seed 50001 (two intake window fans against one small open window, first blamed on the fan curve's steep slope near stall, which changed nothing once the operator was right) and one-bedroom seeds 1004 and 1005 at 0.1 and 0.075 m. A coarse correction scaled by 2 reproduces the old failures exactly, and scales from 1 to 1.75 are all stable. Three cycles stay the default.
   - The averaging of the last two pressure solves (the first fix) stays as a second line of defence: without it, two-bedroom seed 52004 (two fans and a purifier in an 8 m/s wind) ran away at 0.075 m.
   - What remains: the solve converges only 0.73 to 0.97 per cycle (the old operator measured 0.54 to 0.87 by residual norm, but flipped sign), because aggregates straddle thin walls. Two cycles leave about three times the divergence of three. A faster, more accurate solve needs aggregates that follow the walls, or conjugate gradients with this cycle as the preconditioner, on the CPU and the GPU.
   - Numbers: the cavity, Nusselt, decay and through-flow results did not move. The free jet reads exponent −0.93 and K 6.15 (originally −1.05 and 5.85, and −0.88 and 6.23 with the averaging alone), so K sits 1% above the published 5.7 to 6.1, inside the test's band. `shearCoefficient` (0.015) was tuned against the old solver and can be retuned.
   - Hang risk: the CPU's scalar substep count had no cap, so a blown-up case took minutes per step, which would freeze the page's CPU fallback. It is capped at 64 like the GPU's. The page should also catch a runaway (a non-finite value or air faster than about 40 m/s in `diagnostics`) and offer a reset, not draw garbage.

A purifier's CADR at each speed is its top-speed figure times that speed's power mark in `PURIFIER_MARKS` (`devices/model.ts`: Sleep 0.25, Low 0.5, Medium 0.75, High 1), so CADR is taken as proportional to fan speed, because no maker, AHAM or ENERGY STAR source publishes any speed but the highest. Each purifier's `source` line says what was read, what was derived and what was estimated, the explainer's "What it isn't" does not say so yet (#57 criterion 4), and the Dock says so beside a chosen purifier's power. 13. Grid-scale energy growth in the velocity advection: bounded, not removed. Browser QA found that a sealed, isothermal room whose fan had stopped first calmed and then filled with an even, blotchy circulation, in an empty box too (kinetic energy 2.3 then 29 in 80 s, more than the running fan had given it). Measured stage by stage in the staggered grid's own energy (the open-weighted squares of the face velocities, which the pressure projection can only lower), the MacCormack velocity advection added about 1% of the energy a step, the eddy viscosity took most of it back, and 97% of what stayed was at the grid scale (what a 3-cell box average leaves behind). The cell-centred mean speed, which is what a reader sees, understates it, and it was first read as a smooth circulation. Semi-Lagrangian advection decays correctly. Shrinking the correction's weight stops the growth but the benchmarks, tuned at the full weight, notice any change below 0.5 (a weight of 0.45 already fails the lid-driven cavity). So the correction keeps its weight and its excursion is bounded to `ADVECTION_LIMIT` of the way to the extremes of the stencil it was interpolated from (`solver/types.ts`; 1 is the old plain clamp, 0.6 now; the GPU kernel reads the same constant). Scan of that share in the empty box: 0.9 and 0.8 stop the room regrowing but the grid-scale share keeps rising (0.47 to 0.92 at 0.9), 0.7 and 0.6 hold it near 0.4, 0.4 near 0.28, 0.25 near 0.2. Cost: the 0.06 Pa orifice case reads 95.7% of the formula (96% before) and the free jet reads exponent −0.87 and K 6.21, so `shearCoefficient` (0.015, tuned before) can be retuned. The test: `tests/solver/decay.spec.ts` fails at a limit of 1 and passes at 0.6, and the window-fan preset test checks that no room is above 10 cm/s a minute after the fan stops. Tried and not the cause: using the velocity at the forward midpoint for the forward trace. What remains: 0.6 is empirical. The principled fix is a skew-symmetric (energy-conserving) convective term, or a hyperviscosity that acts only on the grid scale; the first changes the GPU kernels and the second adds one. The stress sweeps pass with the limit: the GPU's at every size down to 0.075 m, and the CPU's at 0.2, 0.15 and 0.1 m.

Left from step 2, none blocking: a worker that dies without an error event (the browser kills it) is not noticed, so its steps stay unfinished, nothing more is sent, `physics.rate` decays to 0 and the status keeps saying "Running" (a watchdog belongs with the runaway guard, #59); a GPU adapter that is a software fallback is used like any other and could be slower than the worker (the tuner, #56, should measure it); if the device fails while the solver is being built on it, that one world runs on the worker at the GPU's grid, 0.15 m, which is slow (34 to 109 ms a step) until the next world; a lost device starts the world again from rest, because nothing can be read back from it, and the page stays on the worker from then on; the worker's 0.2 m cells were timed only on this Mac, so whether a typical phone keeps up is unmeasured (#64). The rate the page shows is simulated time sent per drawn-frame time, and a frame's budget is capped at 0.1 s, so a page whose frames stall runs slower than the number says (Chromium's software-rendered headless shell at 17 to 25 fps: 1.67 simulated seconds per wall second against 2.14 shown for the studio at 20×; steps go out only per drawn frame, so the worker idles there).
